About the Balancer

A guide to the balancer, its ratings, and the data behind them. Open any technical note for the exact rules.

Purpose Main Elo Civ bias Inactivity Balancing Rating modes
01

What the site does

The Hobbit Balancer turns recorded community matches into player ratings, then uses those ratings to create fair 4v4 teams and suitable position assignments. It also keeps match history, player profiles, live import status, and community statistics in one place.

Technical model and boundaries

Eight selected players are divided between four Evil positions (P1-P4) and four Good positions (P5-P8). A player's permanent skill, position-specific history, inactivity, and assignment restrictions influence the result in different ways.

The site intentionally has several Elo-like values. They answer different questions:

  • Main Elo: permanent result-based skill.
  • Civ Elo: permanent Main Elo plus a non-positive civ bias.
  • Displayed Elo: Main Elo after full inactivity decay.
  • Balancer Elo: Civ Elo plus the hard-position adjustment and 60% of inactivity decay.
02

Main Elo

Main Elo is the player's permanent, position-independent rating. It changes after a completed match according to the strength of both teams and whether the player won or lost.

Formula, expected result, and K-factor

Team strength is the average permanent Main Elo of its four players. Expected score uses the standard logistic Elo curve:

expected = 1 / (1 + 10((opponent average - team average) / 400))
Main Elo change = round(K x (actual result - expected))

The actual result is 1 for a win and 0 for a loss. Newer players move faster: base K is 40 below 10 games, 32 below 30 games, and 24 thereafter. Compared with the community's average game count, K is multiplied by 1 up to 1.5x the average, 0.9 up to 2.5x, 0.8 up to 4x, and 0.7 above 4x. The final K is constrained to 16-40.

A match changes ratings only when at least one registered player can be matched on each team. Matches without rated players on both sides remain in History and are marked Unrated.

Ratings never fall below 100.

03

Civilization and position bias

Every player has exactly two positions with zero bias: their best current options are equal to their Main Elo. Every other position has a negative bias, so a civ can never raise a player's permanent rating above their Main Elo.

How win rate, experience, and the two zero biases are calculated

Each position uses only its recorded games and wins. Every position begins with a 20-point baseline penalty. Fewer than five games add an inexperience penalty: 0 games = 20, 1 = 15, 2 = 10, 3 = 5, 4 = 3, and 5 or more = 0.

A win-rate penalty applies below 50%. It rises slowly for ordinary results and accelerates for very low win rates:

deficit = max(0, (0.50 - civ win rate) / 0.50)
win-rate penalty = round(200 x deficit1.5)
raw penalty = 20 + inexperience penalty + win-rate penalty

All eight raw penalties are ranked after both experience and win rate are included. If penalties tie, the higher win rate ranks first, followed by more games. The best two positions are forced to zero bias. Every other position receives its negative raw penalty, constrained to a maximum loss of 200 Elo.

Civ Elo = permanent Main Elo + civ bias

The final civ bias is always between 0 and -200. Old modifier values may still exist in saved legacy data, but they are not used by this calculation.

04

Inactivity and displayed Elo

Long inactivity temporarily lowers the Elo shown in rankings. It does not erase the player's permanent Main Elo, and active players can clear the penalty by returning to play.

Decay curve, recovery, and where it applies

There is no penalty before 7 inactive days. At day 7 the penalty begins at 50 Elo, rises linearly, and reaches 300 Elo at day 30. It does not grow beyond 300.

Displayed Elo = max(100, permanent Main Elo - active inactivity penalty)
Displayed civ ranking = max(100, Displayed Elo + civ bias)

When a decayed player returns, the current penalty is banked. It clears after two games played within a seven-day recovery window. Until then, it remains visible.

The balancer applies only 60% of the active inactivity penalty. This deliberately keeps returning players from being treated as fully 300 Elo weaker when creating teams.

05

How teams are balanced

The balancer considers every 4v4 team split and every legal position assignment. It favors close team strength while also respecting bans, inexperience, and recent position repetition.

Candidate search, assignment Elo, and score

There are 70 ways to choose four of eight players for Evil. For each split, all 24 Evil and 24 Good position permutations are evaluated, producing 40,320 complete candidates before banned assignments are removed.

Balancer Elo = Civ Elo + hard-civ penalty - round(inactivity penalty x 0.6)

P2 and P6 receive a difficulty penalty for lower-rated players. The benchmark is the rounded average Main Elo of the top five players in the active rating mode. The penalty begins below 70% of that benchmark and scales to -120 at 40% of it. For example, a 2000-Elo benchmark produces a 1400 start and an 800 full-penalty point.

Assignment selection has a separate penalty score: zero games +20 and repeating the immediately previous civ +30. A banned civ makes that assignment illegal.

candidate score = abs(Evil total - Good total) + assignment penalties

Balanced mode randomly selects among candidates within 60 points of the best score, avoiding the immediately previous exact result when possible. Random mode widens that range to 200 and tries to produce a different team split.

06

Original and 1000 Replay modes

The rating-mode control compares community-assigned starting ratings with an equal 1000-Elo start. Both modes replay the same recorded matches and change the active player dataset across the entire site.

How both datasets are built

Original resets every player to the starting Elo assigned in the community rating sheet, clears their results, and replays all recorded matches in timestamp order. A future player without a sheet rating starts at 900.

1000 Replay resets every player to 1000 Main Elo with no recorded games, then replays all recorded matches in timestamp order through the current rating formulas.

Both modes use the same match and rating calculations. Only the starting Elo differs. Inactivity state is reconstructed from the same history after the replay.
